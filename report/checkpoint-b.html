1. Introduction — what I want to solve

My aim is to grow wealth over many years, participate in rising markets and understand the losses I may face. This study chooses ETFs and weights only for the growth block. The safety and inflation blocks stay outside its decisions, collateral and financing.

The first users are investors like me: people who spend in reais, protect dated expenses separately and want the remaining capital to grow without picking individual stocks. The application is the program that applies the allocation rule.

The question is whether controlled short sales improve net results over the same long-only rule, without hiding concentration or leverage. Long market histories matter because a rule that works during an expansion may fail in a downturn.

2. Literature review — what supports the rule

Markowitz connects weights, expected returns and covariance. Jorion motivates reducing error in estimated means; Ledoit–Wolf does this for the risk matrix. Rockafellar and Uryasev provide the CVaR framework for controlling modeled tail losses. Company classifications use issuer records and the available sector hierarchy.

The previous assignment found higher maximum returns when shorts were allowed, together with higher risk and incomplete costs. This study adds borrowing, financing and collateral. None of these references establishes a universally optimal concentration percentage.

3. Methods — the rule in a few steps

Of 61 candidates, 46 have complete source files; 44 meet the equity-coverage requirement. At the latest decision, 35 pass the filters and 29 remain after selecting one representative for equivalent exposures. Eligibility requires 60 complete monthly returns and trailing six-month median daily dollar volume of at least US$1 million, with at least 100 observations.

Adjusted prices and exchange rates come from Yahoo Finance, with a cutoff of October 23, 2026 and complete months through September. Holdings come from Vanguard, BlackRock, State Street and CVM. Distributions are already reflected in adjusted prices and are not added twice. Price movements are described by 100 × ln(Pt/Pt−1); wealth and weights use simple USD returns.

There are no 25 common years of actual data for this rule and selected universe. The July 2019–September 2026 experiment includes COVID and 2022, but not the 2001 or 2008 crises.

These are study policies. A 5% company position limits the direct impact of an isolated 50% shock to 2.5 percentage points of wealth; other assets may fall together. Share classes are combined by issuer, but wider corporate groups need further mapping. A security’s quotation currency is an exchange-rate proxy. Monthly CVaR is not a maximum drawdown guarantee.

w: weights; μ: expected returns; rf: cash rate; Σ: covariance; A: underlying exposures; b: limits; C: costs; s/ℓ: short/long bounds; G: gross-exposure cap.

Sharpe measures return above cash per unit of volatility. Shrinkage reduces confidence in noisy estimates. CVaR measures average loss in the bad tail of the scenarios. Means use an adaptation inspired by Bayes–Stein; covariance uses Ledoit–Wolf.

The signal is monthly. Orders wait for the first later day with observed quotes for the ETFs involved. Existing holdings may carry a prior quote for valuation only, for at most 14 calendar days, with quote age recorded. Three-percentage-point bands use the decision snapshot, never the execution-day return. If no positive expected excess return is feasible, the rule uses constrained minimum variance.

Limits apply to the target and monthly rebalance; prices may cause drift between decisions. Simplified maintenance margin is monitored daily.

Assumed costs are 0.10% of traded value, 0.25% annual management, 2% annual borrowing on shorts, and financing at cash plus one percentage point. Collateral is separate from debt and earns no rebate. Projected monthly trading cost is annualized in the objective; the account charges only costs incurred. Internal fund expenses already reflected in prices are not deducted again. Actual borrow availability, spreads and individual taxes remain unverified.

Against VT over the same period, long/short regression alpha is 0.53 percentage points per year and beta is 0.85. These are descriptive historical estimates, not causal effects or evidence of skill.

Converted to reais, the portfolio’s nominal CAGR is 16.78%, with a maximum drawdown of -31.77%. This is the same portfolio translated through FX, before inflation and individual taxes. BRL Sharpe is not computed against US cash.

5. Conclusions — what the evidence supports

The proposal is the ETF portfolio found for block C, with the risks and costs of maintaining it. Benchmarks measure its advantage; missing a performance target does not automatically replace the mandate with one ETF.

Short sales did not demonstrate a net improvement over the long-only version in this test. The Checkpoint A target of a 0.1 Sharpe advantage over both VT and equal weight was not demonstrated in the history or replay. It appears in 28.0% of dynamic paths: a conditional simulation frequency, not a future success probability. Shorts are not forced to change this conclusion.

Underlying concentration limits improve on ticker-only limits. The evidence remains research evidence: complete historical holdings and actual borrowing terms are still needed for operational validation. A rule can compound well and still lose heavily in a crisis.

For Checkpoint C, the next steps are historical snapshots or prospective validation using then-known holdings, confirmation of borrowing and margin by market, and continued net comparison with the long-only rule. A launch decision should wait for that work.
